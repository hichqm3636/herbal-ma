# Herbal.ma Editorial Policy

## Purpose

Herbal.ma publishes educational health and wellness content. Accuracy, proportionality, transparency, and reader safety take priority over publishing speed, search traffic, automation output, or commercial opportunity.

## Evidence hierarchy

Evidence should be selected according to the claim being made. In general, prefer:

1. official guidance and primary materials from recognized public-health, regulatory, and scientific institutions;
2. current evidence syntheses, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and evidence-based clinical guidelines;
3. peer-reviewed controlled trials and other primary research appropriate to the question;
4. reputable academic, professional, and reference materials used for explanation or context;
5. manufacturer or commercial materials only for attributable product facts, never as sole support for independent health-effect claims.

A higher position does not automatically make a source suitable. Relevance, recency, methods, population, limitations, and conflicts of interest must be assessed.

## Source quality rules

- Every material health claim must be traceable to suitable evidence.
- Sources must support the precise claim and degree of certainty expressed.
- Prefer original or authoritative sources over summaries when practical.
- Do not rely on AI output, search-result snippets, anonymous content, promotional copy, or unsupported social content as evidence.
- Distinguish evidence in humans from laboratory, animal, mechanistic, or traditional-use evidence.
- Do not generalize beyond the studied dose, formulation, population, outcome, or duration.
- Report material uncertainty, conflicting evidence, limitations, and relevant dates.
- Broken, retracted, superseded, or materially disputed sources require review and possible correction or archival action.

## Authorship transparency

Published content must identify the accountable human Author. Where separate review is required, the Reviewer and relevant review role must also be visible or otherwise recorded transparently.

Qualifications and affiliations must be described accurately and without inflation. Material conflicts of interest and commercial relationships must be disclosed. AI must not be presented as an author or accountable reviewer.

## Medical and health risk levels

Every health-related entry must be classified as Low, Medium, or High risk according to [CONTENT_MODEL.md](CONTENT_MODEL.md).

- **Low:** standard editorial and source review.
- **Medium:** explicit human review of each consequential claim and its supporting evidence.
- **High:** enhanced review by a human with appropriate subject-matter competence before approval.

Risk classification may be raised at any point. Uncertainty is resolved toward the safer review level.

## Review expectations

Review must cover, as relevant:

- factual accuracy and faithful representation of sources;
- evidence strength, uncertainty, and limitations;
- health-risk classification;
- absence of diagnosis, personalized treatment, or unsupported dosing advice;
- language clarity and cultural suitability;
- Arabic and English meaning where translated counterparts exist;
- commercial disclosures;
- metadata, dates, authorship, links, and citations;
- whether information could cause a reader to delay or replace professional care.

Translation is editorial work. A translated entry needs its own review and approval; approval of one locale does not approve the other.

## AI-assisted, human-approved rule

AI may assist with research organization, outlines, drafting, translation, editing suggestions, metadata candidates, internal-link candidates, and review checklists.

AI must not:

- make the final publication decision;
- be treated as evidence;
- invent or conceal sources;
- bypass risk classification or required review;
- autonomously publish, update, translate, or archive health content;
- convert promotional claims into editorial claims without independent evidence.

A named human must review and approve the exact content version before it becomes publishable. Automation must preserve an auditable distinction between generated, reviewed, approved, and published states.

## Publication workflow

1. **Define:** identify the reader need, scope, locale, and preliminary risk level.
2. **Research:** collect suitable sources and record important limitations or conflicts.
3. **Draft:** prepare the entry; AI assistance is allowed within this policy.
4. **Review:** verify evidence, claims, language, disclosures, links, and risk classification.
5. **Approve:** an accountable human approves the exact candidate. High-risk material receives the required enhanced review.
6. **Publish:** only approved content is made public.
7. **Monitor:** record corrections, new evidence, source changes, and review triggers.
8. **Update or archive:** revise with renewed approval or withdraw from normal discovery when it can no longer be responsibly maintained.

A successful build or deployment never substitutes for steps 4 and 5.

## Updates, corrections, and archival

- Show meaningful publication and last-reviewed or updated dates.
- Correct material errors promptly and transparently at a level proportionate to their potential harm.
- Re-review content when important evidence, official guidance, regulation, safety information, or linked source status changes.
- Update claims without erasing the fact that an earlier version existed when the change is material.
- Mark content as needing update when concerns are known but a safe revision is not yet approved.
- Archive content that is obsolete, unsafe, unsupported, duplicative, or no longer maintainable. Archival must not create misleading redirects.

Exact review intervals by topic and risk level remain an editorial operating decision to be confirmed before routine publication.

## Unsupported health claims

Herbal.ma prohibits unsupported, exaggerated, or misleading claims about preventing, diagnosing, treating, or curing disease. It also prohibits certainty that exceeds the evidence, guaranteed outcomes, fabricated consensus, decontextualized statistics, and omission of material risks or limitations.

Educational disclaimers do not make an unsupported claim acceptable.

## Commercial transparency and Herbialife

Herbialife is an external commercial brand related to Herbal.ma's founders. That relationship must be disclosed wherever it may reasonably affect reader interpretation.

Any product link, mention, placement, sponsorship, affiliate arrangement, or commercial benefit must be clearly labeled. Herbialife materials may support attributable facts about a Herbialife product but cannot serve as the sole evidence for independent health-effect claims.

## Educational independence

Editorial topics, conclusions, risk statements, and evidence assessments must not be shaped to promote Herbialife products or Clinora listings. Educational pages must remain useful even when no related product is available or linked.

Commercial calls to action, if approved later, must be visually and semantically separate from editorial evidence. No commercial relationship may override correction, review, disclosure, or archival requirements.
